# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Technical collaborators, open-source maintainers, AI researchers, community builders, hiring leaders, and prospective partners visiting Rudy Celekli's GitHub profile. They need to understand his work, operating philosophy, and demonstrated contribution quickly without reading an entire résumé.

## Product Purpose

Present an evidence-first professional identity around trustworthy agentic systems. Success means a visitor can distinguish named, officially verified upstream contributions from privacy-preserving aggregate work, understand Rudy's approach to agentic engineering, and inspect the evidence and assumptions behind material claims.

## Brand Personality

Ambitious, technical, and accountable. The voice is confident about demonstrated outcomes, precise about uncertainty, visually distinctive, and willing to say when a measurement is modeled rather than observed.

## Anti-references

- Generic badge walls, résumé dumps, or template-like developer profiles.
- Unattributed projects, implied ownership, or borrowed visual language presented as original work.
- Vanity metrics that count drafts, open pull requests, tokens, or activity as accepted impact.
- Dense diagrams with clipped labels, overflow, or unreadable type at GitHub's rendered width.
- Unsupported Agentic Power, savings, ROI, or human-replacement claims.

## Design Principles

- Proof before prominence: the strongest claims expose their evidence and counting method.
- Make systems legible: diagrams should explain an operating model in one scan.
- Separate ownership, contribution, and experimentation explicitly.
- Use visual intensity to direct attention, not to camouflage uncertainty.
- Preserve the human role: goals, judgment, acceptance, and accountability remain visible.
- Use motion to explain flow and state change, never to distract from evidence or caveats.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target readable contrast, meaningful SVG titles and descriptions, text alternatives for visual statistics, keyboard-safe links, reduced-motion fallbacks, and layouts that remain legible when GitHub scales assets down on desktop or mobile.
